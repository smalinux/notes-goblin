# Direction 02 — The Chess-Player's CTF Ladder

**Pitch:** You don't play video games, but you play chess. CTF pwn/RE *is* chess for binaries: a finite board, near-perfect information, and one elegant line that wins.

## Why this fits you

Everything you love about chess is here — a bounded puzzle, a rating/ranking, asynchronous play, and the deep satisfaction of a clean line. It also forces your assembly and GDB skills to get fast and real under pressure, which no book ever will. Treat solves like games: play first, review after.

## The ladder (rated, like chess)

1. **picoCTF** — gentle on-ramp; do the Reverse Engineering and Binary Exploitation categories. *(skills 4, 11, 15)*
2. **crackmes.one** — easy, then medium. Pure RE tactics training. *(skills 15, 16, 21)*
3. **pwn.college** (you already have it listed) — the structured dojo. Work Program Interaction → Memory Errors → Shellcode modules in order. *(skills 11, 25)*
4. **Nightmare / guyinatuxedo** (already listed) — curated pwn problems with scaffolding. *(skill 25)*
5. **pwnable.kr / pwnable.tw** — classic, elegant, a real rating ladder. *(skills 19, 25, 26)*
6. **Live CTFs via CTFtime** — join or form a team, play weekend events. The clock and the scoreboard are the adrenaline that beats boredom.

## The discipline that makes it stick

For every solve, write a short writeup — this is your *opening repertoire*. Keep one running `patterns.md` of recurring tricks (ret2libc, format-string write, tcache poisoning) — the finite set of tactical motifs, exactly like chess tactics. Only read someone else's writeup *after* a genuine attempt, the way you review a game with an engine after you've played it yourself.

## Answer key

Writeups exist for almost every retired challenge — but gate them behind your own attempt, or you're just watching chess instead of playing it.
