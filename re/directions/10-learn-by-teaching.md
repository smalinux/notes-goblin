# Direction 10 — Learn by Teaching: Crackme Authoring, Writeups & Portfolio

**Pitch:** You clearly love curating notes-goblin — turn that instinct into the fastest known learning multiplier: teach it.

## Why this fits you

You're already a documenter: the steppers, the roadmaps, the YouTube vault. Producing output forces mastery (you can't explain what you don't understand), it kills boredom because you're *making* instead of *consuming*, and it builds the public portfolio the companies in your `jobs/` folder are explicitly looking for. You already publish notes-goblin to GitHub Pages — the distribution channel exists.

## The ladder

1. **First-hour win — author a crackme.** A simple license check in C, stripped, posted on crackmes.one. Then read how strangers solve the thing you built. *(skills 16, 21)*
2. **Escalate the challenge.** Add layers — an XOR'd string, a tiny bytecode VM, an anti-debug check. To *design* a technique you're forced to master it. *(skills 20, 21)*
3. **Writeups as default output.** Every binary you solve (CTF, sample, crackme) gets a short public writeup. Start a blog on the Pages site you already run. *(skill 29)*
4. **Interactive explainers.** Use your HTML-stepper style to build explainers for PLT/GOT, the heap, ROP — aimed outward this time, at other learners. You already do this better than most; publish it.
5. **Give a talk.** A local meetup or a 10-minute recorded screencast reversing something. Explaining on camera exposes every gap you've been papering over.
6. **Capstone.** A mini "RE course" or a challenge series under notes-goblin, or a writeup contributed to a well-known repo. This is a direct hiring signal for security firms.

## Answer key

The community solving your crackmes *is* the feedback loop. For structure and voice, study how the best writeups are built (0xdf, LiveOverflow) and model them.

## Tools

Your existing HTML/markdown toolchain, GitHub Pages, crackmes.one, asciinema for terminal screencasts.

## Channels (already in your vault)

@LiveOverflow, @0xdf, @stacksmashing — all masters of the explain-it-clearly craft worth modeling.

**The fun hook:** watching someone else solve the puzzle *you* built, and the first "this writeup is what finally made it click for me" comment.
