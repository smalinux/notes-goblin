
1. **The Art of Software Security Assessment** — Dowd, McDonald, Schuh. Twenty years old and still the best book on finding bugs by reading C. This is the one that matters most for you.
2. **Blue Fox: Arm Assembly Internals and Reverse Engineering** — Maria Markstedter. Modern, and ARM is your native architecture. Rare combination.
3. **Practical Binary Analysis** — Dennis Andriesse. Linux-native binary instrumentation, taint analysis, symbolic execution.
4. **The Ghidra Book** — Eagle & Nance. Learn Ghidra rather than paying for IDA at the start.
5. **A Guide to Kernel Exploitation** — Perla & Oldani. The only book-length treatment of kernel exploitation. Badly dated (2010, pre-SMEP/SMAP/KASLR) — read it for structure, get current technique from kernelCTF writeups.
6. **The Shellcoder's Handbook, 2nd ed.** — Also dated, also foundational. Skim.
7. **Attacking Network Protocols** — James Forshaw. Excellent, and Forshaw is worth reading on anything.
8. **Rootkits and Bootkits** — Matrosov, Rodionov, Bratus. Firmware and bootloader attack surface. Directly adjacent to your barebox and U-Boot work — you'd read this faster than almost anyone.
9. **Serious Cryptography, 2nd ed.** — Aumasson. Crypto bugs are everywhere and most people miss them.
10. **Practical Reverse Engineering** — Dang, Gazet, Bachaalany. Broad coverage including ARM and kernel internals.

**Free and better than most of the above:** *The Fuzzing Book* (online), the Google Project Zero blog archive read as a curriculum, Phrack, and the `google/security-research` repo.
